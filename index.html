Module 2

My Activity Research

In Workshop 1, I made a stand out of cardboard to explore how screens are placed and fixed.
In Workshop 2, I brainstormed the idea of the humidity lamp and made an arched cardboard prototype. I cut out irregular observation Windows on the front side and used fish school photos to simulate the picture of the Project 1 concept to explore how the original fish school concept can be combined with the physical lights.
In Activity 1, I mainly tested the original fish projection scheme. Since TouchDesigner wasn't working as well as expected, I developed an alternative based on Workshop 2: a tablet or computer display of the shoals, with a physical humidity light.
In Activity 2, I made the switch page with ProtoPie, and I wrote the code to control the lights and detect the humidity in the Arduino IDE, which I am still debugging. I am also continuing to explore how to integrate sensor data into TouchDesigner and combine it with the original fish screen to gradually improve the interactive prototype.

Project 2

Project 2 Prototype

Plan 1 is the main direction in which the Project 2 prototype continues to develop. The left image shows me testing the gesture and fish screen in TouchDesigner. I hope that as you clench your left hand, the fish will emerge from the shadows. When the audience opens their right hand and moves their palm, the fish will follow the palm and change the swimming direction. With these two movements, I want the audience to first detect the school of fish from the dark, and then use hand gestures to guide them to swim.
Next, I will continue to debug gesture recognition and the fish animation, focusing on whether the two actions can stably trigger the corresponding screen. I will also continue to adjust the projection to see if the fish emerge clearly from the dark and if the projection gives me the dark sea atmosphere I want.

Plan 2 (Alternative): Physical humidity lamp
The image on the right shows an alternative for when the drop shadow is still not as good as expected. If I used this solution, I would try to display TouchDesigner's dynamic school of fish on a tablet or computer and keep the original gesture interaction. The components in the diagram are planned combinations.
1. Scrub or clear front plate
The arched frame and irregular opening of the cardboard model continue, allowing the audience to look underwater through a window. I will compare the two materials to see which one can soften the light while preserving the clarity of the fish.
2. Clear fillets
Add a fixed silhouette of the school of fish on the transparent sheet to form a front and back layer with the school of fish swimming in the rear screen.
3. LED lights
Put the LED behind the transparent fish and in front of the screen. I will first use a monochrome LED to test whether it can be switched on and off according to the humidity reading, and then consider using different colors to correspond to different humidity ranges.
Step 4: Screen
Display TouchDesigner's dynamic school of fish on a tablet or computer, allowing viewers to see the picture up close through a window.
5. Arduino Sensor Kit
The Kit with the humidity sensor is placed behind the screen, and a wire is connected to the LED in the front, leaving room for the fish and lights on the front. 6. Rear shell and vent

In the future, if I use 3D modeling to make the shell, I will reserve a vent near the sensor and compare the humidity readings before and after loading the shell to determine the location and size of the opening.
